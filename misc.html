Biography

My freshman/sophomore was community college, no math classes beyond linear algebra, but I was deciphering Clay math millenium problems, and no outstanding record on Putnam. My undergraduate junior/senior is all about studying Girillet (abstract algebra) preparing Hartshorne or Alan Hatcher, and I obtained a strong recommendation, while I didn’t take much class of analysis/physics. My first PhD is top 50-100 in US news math ranking, but there is a solid research group of mirror symmetry, a huge subject intersecting the work of roughly half of Fields medal recipients. Because of heavy workload, doing good PhD requires a solid outline of research proposal by admission so to graduate in time.

Below is a book review if that helps. For starting a new PhD, I recommend a book titled ”A Mathematician’s Survial Guide” https://www.amazon.com/Mathematicians-Survival-Guide-Graduate-Development/dp/082183455X, and this book says the system of grad school of qualifying exams and thesis defense, and PhD admission and postgraduation career path. I’d also recommend a free online link ”Postdoc Career Advice” https://math.ou.edu/ kmartin/career-ad.html.

 

Philosophy

There’s no answer in philosophy, but what I’d value in philosophy is the clarity of problem setting, accessibility to the audience, and extendability by endless curiousity, and furthermore the good way of doing philosophy is how to interpret it not what exactly is said, since the author might be lying or mistaking, while conversely, good thinking can naturally debug mistake, and lying is like hacking contest. The great philsophies are Darwin, UNIX, and Newton to take an example, while for statistics and quantum mechanics and Marxism, statistics can count GDP but it doesn’t explain the value of service industry. and nobody understand what exactly Marx said, and QFT model doesn’t explain the actual meaning of quantum mechanics. Math in my opinion is a great kind of philosophy. The modern math is Bourbaki style, but it’s super clear if dictionary is unlimitedly allowed, easy to find open question, and the research is unfinished. The good analogy is that math is like climbing a mountain. Skilled climber says climb up to the notch rather than climb down if we’re lost, and abstract nonsense is like the apex of the mountain.

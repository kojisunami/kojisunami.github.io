\noindent
\textbf{Philosophy} \\

\noindent
What I'd value in philosophy is the clarity of problem setting, accessibility to the audience, and extendability by endless curiousity, 
and furthermore the ability to judge what is said and to correct it if needed in case the author is lying or mistaking.
Naturally, the goodness of philosophy depends on user.
The great philosophies are Darwin, UNIX, and Newton for their clarify, popularity, and freedom,
but statistics, Marxism, and quantum mechanics might be different, since statistics doesn't explain the meaning of service industry.
and nobody understand what exactly Marx said (I don't say if it's wrong) or epistemology part of quantum mechanics.
Math might be a great philosophy if it has 1: clarify if unlimited use of dictionary is allowed, 
2: accessibility of everybody of finding a meaning itself, 3: depth of research. Here is an analogy, math is like climbing a mountain,
and skilled climber says climb up to the notch rather than climb down if we're lost, and abstract nonsense is the apex of the mountain.

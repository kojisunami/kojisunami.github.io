\noindent
\textbf{Philosophy} \\

\noindent
There's no answer in philosophy, but what I'd value in philosophy is the clarity of problem setting, accessibility to the audience,
and extendability by endless curiousity, and furthermore the good way of doing philosophy is not what exactly is said but how to interpret it,
since the author might be lying or mistaking, but good thinking can debug mistake, and lying is like hacking contest,
because human can lie but language cannot lie. 
To pick some examples, the great philosophies are Darwin, UNIX, and Newton for clarify, popularity, and freedom,
while for statistics, Marxism, and quantum mechanics, statistics can count GDP but it doesn't explain the value of service industry.
and nobody understand what exactly Marx said, and QFT model doesn't explain the actual meaning of quantum mechanics. 
Math in my opinion is a great kind of philosophy, since math is super clear if dictionary is unlimitedly allowed, 
easy to find open question, and the research is unfinished. The good analogy is that math is like climbing a mountain.
Skilled climber says climb up to the notch rather than climb down if we're lost, and abstract nonsense is the apex of the mountain.

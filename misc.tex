\textbf{Philosophy} \\

\noindent
What I'd value in philosophy is the clarity of problem setting, accessibility to the audience, and extendability by endless curiousity, 
and furthermore the ability to judge what is said and to correct it if needed in case the author is lying or mistaking,
and Darwin, UNIX, and Newton: where goodness doesn't mean impeccability but the ability of customization whenever necessary,
and what we are aiming in math is its clarity with unlimited use of dictionary, 
typically Bourbaki style (Russel is more precise but Bourbaki is enough),
accessibility to physics or undergraduate math or general audience, and stir unlimited research mind to avoid boredom.
Math is like climbing a mountain, and skilled climber says climb up to the notch rather than climb down if we're lost, 
and abstract nonsense is the apex of the mountain -- From Grothendieck To Einstein. \\

\noindent
What I'd value in logic is not to behave logically. Human is infallible in logics, and Rock-paper-scissors isn't syllogism.
Furthermore, "not" is a useless word, since it doesn't help understanding of non-abelian because of abelian,
but 2-torus is how to memorize what non-abelian is by common sense.
